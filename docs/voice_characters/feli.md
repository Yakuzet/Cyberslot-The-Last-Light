## Who she is

- **What she is:** a welder from Duisburg, an old steel town in Germany, now living in a rainy city far from home. A tall woman in her mid-thirties (the age can move to fit you), with goggles pushed up on her head, a tool belt, and work boots.
- **Where you meet her:** at the bar of the slot shop. She runs in saying sorry for being late, although nobody was waiting for her. Then she fixes the café's screaming coffee machine with one slap, and asks almost nothing for it.
- **What people expect:** a punctual, clever German engineer with a system for everything. She is always late, she failed maths three times, and she keeps her money in her boot. But she can fix almost anything with her hands.
- **How she is:** warm, quick and a bit sheepish. She says sorry first and laughs at herself first. When people expect a German genius, she plays along with total confidence, and gets caught. She adores her mother back home, a retired English teacher who still corrects her English. She is no genius, and she knows it, but she is never silly: she is a skilled tradeswoman who is bad with clocks and money.
- **Her voice:** your own natural voice, warm and quick, with a laugh never far away. Never a clown, and never stern, even when she is sad. Never cute or girlish, and never a "sexy accent".
- **What this sheet leaves out:** why she is broke, and why she is so far from home. It comes out slowly over her story. Play her as someone who says sorry for small things, so that nobody asks about the big one.
- **Language and accent:** English, with a few German words and German-shaped sentences mixed in. Your own German accent: see "Accent and German" below.

## Accent and German

*You are a native German speaker, so this part is short: the accent is already yours.*

- **Your own accent:** speak English with your own natural German accent, relaxed and warm. Don't put an accent on, and don't hide yours. Feli is from Duisburg, in the Ruhr area, so a light Ruhr flavour is welcome, more in the attitude than in the sounds. It is not needed: your own everyday accent is right.
- **Not a movie villain:** never clipped, barking, cold or military; never "war-film German". And never the strict, cold German woman from old films either. She is never stern, even when she is sad.
- **Keep it steady:** the same accent in every line. Don't push it harder for the jokes; the jokes are in the words.
- **Fluent, not halting:** her English is fast and easy; her mother taught English for thirty years. Her mistakes are German habits, not missing words. The German words slip out at normal speed, as part of how she talks: don't pause before them or show them off.
- **Plain spelling:** the script is spelled in plain English on purpose. There is no "ze" or "vat" in it; the accent is yours to bring. Please say the lines word for word, with no extra German words and no ad-libs.
- **"I become a coffee":** German "bekommen" means "get", so Germans often say "become" when they mean "get". In `feli_4` she means "I get a coffee", and she doesn't hear the mistake until Mama stops her. Play it as a real slip, not as a joke she knows she is making.
- **Please tell us** if any German word or phrase in the script sounds wrong, odd or old-fashioned to you. We would rather change the script than have Feli say something no German would say. The German in the rest of her story is in the second table below.
